# INC-2026-006 Line 3 stopped at night — compressed-air leak, compressor at its limit

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: night shift leader, D.N.

## 1. Summary

At 02:40 the Line 3 assembly stations stopped with "air pressure low" one after another, and Line 1
reported clamping faults shortly after. The compressor M-08 itself was running fully loaded and had
no fault: plant air pressure had fallen because a hose on a Line 3 station had burst. Hose replaced;
production restarted at 04:10.

## 2. Timeline

- 02:31: `PRESS-LOW` on `discharge_pressure` of M-08 (6.3 bar).
- 02:40–02:55: Line 3 assembly stations stop one by one (pneumatic tools).
- 03:05: Line 1 machining centers report clamping pressure faults.
- 03:20: loud hissing at Line 3 station 4: burst supply hose.
- 03:35: station isolated; pressure recovers to 7.4 bar within minutes.
- 04:10: hose replaced; all lines running.

## 3. Evidence

- M-08 running 100 % loaded, `motor_current` at the top of its normal range, `discharge_temp` and
  `inlet_filter_dp` normal: the compressor was working at full capacity, not failing
  (MAN-CMP-001 §5.3).
- Several lines affected at nearly the same time: a shared supply problem (MAN-CMP-001 §6).
- Pressure recovered as soon as the leak was isolated.

## 4. Root cause

Aged supply hose on assembly station 4 burst. Demand exceeded what one compressor can deliver.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Hose replaced; all station hoses older than 5 years scheduled for replacement.
- Proposal: a pressure-drop alarm per line, to locate leaks faster.

## 6. Lessons learned

"Why did Line 3 stop?" was answered by the compressor data, but the compressor was not the cause.
When several lines stop together, look at what they share, and then ask why the shared supply failed.
