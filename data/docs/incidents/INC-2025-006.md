# INC-2025-006 M-02 vibration: tool holder suspected, spindle bearing confirmed

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: shift technician, K.M.

## 1. Summary

M-02 showed `VIB-HIGH` alarms over a week. Because a new tool holder batch had arrived the same
week, the first assumption was an unbalanced holder. Cleaning and re-clamping didn't help; a
balanced test arbor showed the same vibration. The spindle bearings were worn. Spindle exchanged
during a planned weekend stop.

## 2. Timeline

- 14 Apr: first `VIB-HIGH` on `spindle_vibration` (2.9 mm/s) shortly after a tool change.
- 15 Apr: taper cleaned, holder re-clamped. Vibration unchanged.
- 16 Apr: three different holders tested: all 2.9–3.1 mm/s. Balanced test arbor: 3.0 mm/s.
- 17 Apr: trend reviewed: vibration had been rising since about 8 April, before the new holders
  arrived. Spindle condition check ordered.
- 22–23 Apr: spindle unit exchanged (SOP-CNC-002).

## 3. Evidence

- A holder problem would make vibration **jump** with a specific holder and stay constant. Here it
  rose **gradually**, independent of the holder, and was the same with a balanced arbor.
- `spindle_bearing_temp` normal throughout (42–51 °C): early stage.
- Rumbling noise at 1 500 rpm, typical of race damage.

## 4. Root cause

Wear of the rear spindle bearing. The timing with the new holder batch was a coincidence.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Spindle exchange per SOP-CNC-002; acceptance passed (0.7 mm/s at 8 000 rpm).
- Test arbor added to the standard toolkit for vibration complaints.

## 6. Lessons learned

Check the trend **before** the most recent change you know about. A test arbor separates "tool
side" from "spindle side" in ten minutes.
