# INC-2024-014 False alarm: M-08 discharge temperature RTD drifting (corroded connector)

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: instrumentation technician, S.L.

## 1. Summary

`TEMP-HIGH` on the discharge temperature of M-08 after a slow rise over two days. Nothing else had
changed: filter dp, current and pressure were normal, and the independent mechanical thermometer
read 84 °C while the RTD showed 97 °C. Corroded connector on the RTD. Cleaned and replaced; no work
on the compressor was needed. A planned compressor stop (and a plant-wide air interruption) was
cancelled.

## 2. Timeline

- 23–25 Nov: `discharge_temp` creeping up by about 6 °C per day, also during the weekend when the
  compressor was mostly unloaded.
- 25 Nov, 14:10: `TEMP-HIGH` (96 °C). Planner prepares an oil-cooler cleaning with a plant air stop.
- 26 Nov, 08:00: desk check per SOP-GEN-004: `inlet_filter_dp` 24 mbar, `motor_current` and
  `discharge_pressure` normal.
- 26 Nov, 09:00: mechanical thermometer 84 °C, contact probe on the pipe 85 °C. RTD 97 °C.
- 26 Nov, 10:30: connector found green with corrosion; RTD resistance measured +5 Ω. Connector and
  probe replaced. Reading 85 °C. Cooler cleaning cancelled.

## 3. Evidence

- Only one signal moved. A real overheating of the airend would show in at least one of filter dp,
  current or pressure, and would follow the load (MAN-CMP-001 §5.2).
- The reading kept rising while the compressor was unloaded at the weekend.
- 12–13 °C difference to two independent reference measurements.

## 4. Root cause

Moisture from the aftercooler condensate drain dripped onto the RTD connector, which corroded and
added contact resistance. A PT100 reads about 2.6 °C high per added ohm.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Connector and probe replaced; drain hose re-routed.
- Data of 23–26 Nov marked as suspect.

## 6. Lessons learned

Check the neighbours before planning the repair. This false alarm almost cost a plant-wide air stop.
