# INC-2025-009 M-08 high discharge temperature traced to the inlet filter

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: shift technician, T.B.

## 1. Summary

In a hot August week, `TEMP-HIGH` on the discharge temperature of M-08 was first blamed on the
weather and the oil cooler. The cooler was clean. The inlet filter differential pressure had been
rising for two days. Clogged filter, cardboard fibres. Element replaced, temperature normal.

## 2. Timeline

- 10 Aug: `TEMP-HIGH` on `discharge_temp` (96 °C) in the afternoon. Outside temperature 33 °C.
- 11 Aug, morning: oil cooler inspected and blown out from the clean side: little dirt. No change.
- 11 Aug, afternoon: trend check: `inlet_filter_dp` 41 → 58 mbar since 9 Aug; `PRESS-HIGH` on it had
  been raised the evening before and not noticed among the temperature alarms.
- 12 Aug: element replaced (SOP-CMP-005); `discharge_temp` back to 88 °C within an hour.

## 3. Evidence

- `inlet_filter_dp`, `discharge_temp` and `motor_current` rising together over two days.
- Oil cooler clean; oil level normal.
- Element covered in brown fibres.

## 4. Root cause

The packaging area had been moved next to the compressor room a month before; cardboard fibres
were drawn into the intake.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Element replaced; intake duct extended to draw air from outside the building.
- Alarm list: `PRESS-HIGH` on `inlet_filter_dp` shown with higher priority.

## 6. Lessons learned

Hot weather was a plausible explanation, which is why nobody looked further for a day. The filter dp
had the answer the whole time.
