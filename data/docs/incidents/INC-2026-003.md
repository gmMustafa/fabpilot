# INC-2026-003 M-09: re-greasing masked a worn bearing for two weeks

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: maintenance supervisor, J.W.

## 1. Summary

After `VIB-HIGH` on M-09 in late February, the bearings were re-greased and the vibration fell back
to normal, so the case was closed. Two weeks later the vibration was back, higher than before, and
`bearing_temp` was rising. The non-drive-end bearing was worn. Replaced in a planned stop with one day
of notice.

## 2. Timeline

- 26 Feb: `VIB-HIGH` on `bearing_vibration` (2.9 mm/s). Bearings re-greased. Next day 1.9 mm/s.
  Closed as "lubrication".
- 9 Mar: vibration back at 2.6 mm/s and rising.
- 14 Mar: `VIB-HIGH` again (3.0), then `VIB-CRIT` (3.5) on 16 Mar.
- 16 Mar: first `TEMP-HIGH` on `bearing_temp` (66 °C).
- 18 Mar: bearings and seal replaced (SOP-PMP-009).

## 3. Evidence

- Vibration rising over weeks, interrupted by one drop right after greasing.
- Temperature rise only in the last days — the late stage of bearing wear (MAN-PMP-001 §5.2).
- Pressures and current normal.

## 4. Root cause

Wear of the non-drive-end bearing 6307. Fresh grease temporarily damped the defect impacts; it did
not repair anything.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Bearings and seal replaced; after 2 h: 0.9 mm/s, 55 °C.
- Rule added: a vibration alarm closed by "re-greasing" gets a follow-up trend check after 7 days.

## 6. Lessons learned

Re-greasing is a valid first step but not a diagnosis. If vibration drops after greasing and creeps
back, the bearing is damaged.
