# INC-2023-009 M-03 coolant interlock trip — chip jammed in the coolant pump

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: shift technician, T.B.

## 1. Summary

M-03 tripped during an aluminium job. Coolant pressure had dropped to zero within a minute and the
spindle temperature rose quickly; the coolant flow interlock stopped the machine. A long chip had
jammed the pump impeller. Downtime 3 h.

## 2. Timeline

- 13:02: `PRESS-LOW` on `coolant_pressure` (0.4 bar).
- 13:03: spindle still cutting dry; `spindle_bearing_temp` climbing.
- 13:12: `TEMP-HIGH` on `spindle_bearing_temp` (57 °C).
- 13:14: `TRIP` (coolant flow interlock).
- 13:30–16:00: pump removed, chip cleared, filter basket replaced, test run.

## 3. Evidence

- Pressure collapse from 4.5 bar to near 0 in under a minute: a pump failure, not a level or filter
  problem (those take hours, SOP-CNC-003 step 1).
- Spindle temperature rose only **after** the pressure loss.
- Spindle vibration normal: the bearings were not the cause.
- Pump shaft blocked by hand; long aluminium chip wrapped around the impeller. Tank filter basket
  torn.

## 4. Root cause

The torn filter basket let a long chip into the pump. Long chips from the new aluminium program
(no chip breaker) made it likely.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Chip removed, basket replaced, pump tested per SOP-CNC-003; pressure 4.6 bar.
- Spindle cooled below 45 °C before restart.
- Program changed to a tool with chip breaker.

## 6. Lessons learned

The order of events tells the story: pressure first, temperature second, trip third. A spindle
running hot with normal coolant pressure would point elsewhere.
