# INC-2023-012 M-05 overload trips during plasticizing — wet regrind

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: process technician, L.K.

## 1. Summary

Injection Molder 1 (M-05) tripped twice in one shift on the pump motor overload relay. Both times
the pump motor current had risen in episodes while the screw slowed down. The material had been
mixed with 30 % regrind straight from the granulator, without drying. Material changed; no further
trips.

## 2. Timeline

- 08:30: start of the new order with 30 % regrind.
- 09:10–10:40: repeated `CURR-HIGH` on `pump_motor_current` (68–72 A), each time with `screw_speed`
  dropping during plasticizing; one `RPM-LOW` (36 rpm).
- 10:52: `CURR-CRIT` (76 A), `TRIP`.
- 11:20: reset by the operator after a cool-down; 12:05 second `TRIP`.
- 12:30: process technician on site; machine stays stopped (SOP-GEN-007: second trip in the shift).
- 13:15: dried material loaded; normal running.

## 3. Evidence

- Current up **while** speed down, in episodes: the overload signature (MAN-IMM-001 §5.1). Normally
  current and speed rise and fall together.
- `barrel_temp_zone2` and `hydraulic_oil_temp` higher during the episodes.
- `hydraulic_pressure` normal: not a hydraulic fault.
- Splay marks (silver streaks) on the parts: typical of moisture in the material.

## 4. Root cause

Undried regrind. Moisture and the cold regrind raised the melt viscosity and the plasticizing
torque beyond what the drive could deliver at speed.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Regrind now goes through the dryer like virgin material; maximum share 20 %.
- Operators briefed on SOP-GEN-007 (the reset at 11:20 should not have happened without a cause).

## 6. Lessons learned

The parts showed the cause before the drive did. An overload on a molder is usually a material or
process question before it is a machine question.
