# INC-2026-902 M-03 spindle bearing damage after a tool collision

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: maintenance engineer, M.K.

## 1. Summary

On 14 August a wrong tool length offset made the spindle of M-03 crash into a fixture. A quick check
afterwards looked fine, and the machine went back into production. The spindle vibration had stepped
up from 0.7 to 1.4 mm/s at the crash and kept growing from there. On 25 August `VIB-HIGH` came, then
`TEMP-HIGH`. Spindle exchanged on 26 August. Only 4.5 years old, the spindle was far from its normal
life.

## 2. Timeline

- 14 Aug, 10:12: collision at rapid feed. Tool and holder broken. Run-out checked: 3 µm (limit 2 µm),
  accepted "for now" because of a rush order.
- 14–24 Aug: `spindle_vibration` rising from 1.4 to 2.7 mm/s, faster each day.
- 25 Aug, 07:30: `VIB-HIGH` (2.9 mm/s); 14:50 `VIB-CRIT` (3.5 mm/s).
- 25 Aug, 16:20: `TEMP-HIGH` on `spindle_bearing_temp` (56 °C). Machine stopped.
- 26 Aug: spindle unit exchanged (SOP-CNC-002).

## 3. Evidence

- A **step** in vibration exactly at the collision, followed by a **growing** trend: damage that
  started at one moment and then progressed like wear.
- Temperature only in the last day, as with any bearing wear (MAN-CNC-001 §5.1).
- Run-out above the limit right after the crash.
- Removed unit: dents (brinelling) on the front bearing races at ball spacing, spalling around them.

## 4. Root cause

The collision dented the bearing races. Every rotation over the dents then caused fatigue spalling,
so the damage grew like normal wear, but starting years too early.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Spindle unit exchanged; acceptance passed (0.6 mm/s at 8 000 rpm).
- Tool length offsets now measured on the presetter and transferred automatically, no manual entry.
- After any collision: run-out **and** a vibration baseline, and a trend check after 3 days before
  the machine counts as OK.

## 6. Lessons learned

"Accepted for now" after a crash was the expensive decision. A collision can start bearing damage that
looks fine on the day and fails ten days later; the vibration trend shows it from the first hour.
