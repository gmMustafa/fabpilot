# INC-2026-005 M-09 falling motor current investigated as an electrical fault — clogged strainer

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: electrician, P.H.

## 1. Summary

The energy monitoring flagged that M-09 drew about 8 % less current than usual. An electrician was
sent to check the motor and the supply. Electrically everything was fine. The strainer dp had risen
for two days: the pump delivered less water and therefore needed less power. Strainer cleaned.

## 2. Timeline

- 25 May: energy report: `motor_current` lower than the weekly average.
- 26 May: motor checked: insulation 450 MΩ, phases balanced, supply fine.
- 26 May, afternoon: utilities technician checks the hydraulic side: `suction_strainer_dp` 0.32 bar
  (`PRESS-HIGH`), `discharge_pressure` 2.7 bar (usually 3.2).
- 27 May: strainer cleaned (SOP-PMP-006): white scale. Current, pressure and dp back to normal.

## 3. Evidence

- Current falling together with discharge pressure while strainer dp rose.
- A centrifugal pump's power follows its flow (MAN-PMP-001 §5.4): less flow, less current.
- No electrical findings.

## 4. Root cause

Hardness scale from the cooling water (water treatment dosing pump had been empty for a week).

## 5. Corrective actions

- Strainer cleaned; dosing pump refilled; low-level alarm on the dosing tank added.

## 6. Lessons learned

Lower current is not automatically an electrical question. On a centrifugal pump it is usually a
flow question: check strainer dp and discharge pressure first.
