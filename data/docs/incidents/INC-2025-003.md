# INC-2025-003 False alarm: M-01 spindle temperature climbing overnight with the machine off

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: maintenance planner, R.S.

## 1. Summary

A spindle exchange for M-01 had already been booked after two days of rising spindle bearing
temperature and a `TEMP-HIGH`. Before the exchange, the instrumentation technician noticed that the
temperature had also risen during the night, with the machine switched off. Reference measurement:
the bearing was at 38 °C, the sensor showed 57 °C. The RTD was replaced; the spindle exchange
(6 h downtime, exchange unit cost) was cancelled.

## 2. Timeline

- 8–10 Feb: `spindle_bearing_temp` drifting upwards about 9 °C per day.
- 10 Feb, 11:20: `TEMP-HIGH` (56 °C). Spindle exchange planned for 12 Feb.
- 11 Feb, 06:30: review of the night: the machine was OFF from 22:00, the reading still rose.
- 11 Feb, 07:15: contact thermometer on the housing 38 °C; RTD 57 °C. PT100 resistance at room
  temperature far off its table value. Probe replaced.

## 3. Evidence

- `spindle_vibration` flat at 1.0–1.2 mm/s for the whole period. Bearing wear raises vibration
  **first** (MAN-CNC-001 §5.1); here vibration never moved.
- `spindle_motor_current` and `coolant_pressure` normal.
- Reading rising with the machine off: a stopped spindle cools down to ambient.

## 4. Root cause

The RTD probe was the original one (8 years) and had aged. Sensor drift; the machine was fine.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Probe replaced per SOP-GEN-004; spindle exchange cancelled.
- Spindle temperature probes added to the yearly verification list for all machining centers.

## 6. Lessons learned

The night readings were the cheapest test available and almost nobody looked at them. "Temperature
alarm on a spindle" does not mean "spindle problem" until a second signal agrees.
