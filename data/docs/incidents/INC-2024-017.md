# INC-2024-017 M-03 spindle overload in a deep pocket — chip packing with a worn end mill

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: shift technician, T.B.

## 1. Summary

M-03 showed repeated spindle current alarms with the spindle slowing down, always in the same
program section (a 60 mm deep pocket). The end mill was at the end of its life and chips were
packing in the pocket. The overload relay tripped once. Tool changed, coolant nozzle re-aimed.

## 2. Timeline

- 18:20–19:45: `CURR-HIGH` on `spindle_motor_current` (39–44 A) in every part, at the same block
  number; `spindle_speed` sagging from 8 000 to about 6 000 rpm during the episodes.
- 19:52: `CURR-CRIT` (47 A), `TRIP` (overload relay).
- 20:10: end mill inspected: worn cutting edges, pocket full of packed chips.
- 20:40: new end mill, nozzle aimed into the pocket, feed reduced by 10 %; normal current.

## 3. Evidence

- Current rising while speed falls, repeating at the same program position (MAN-CNC-001 §5.4).
- `spindle_vibration` and `spindle_bearing_temp` normal: no sign of a spindle bearing problem.
- `coolant_pressure` normal, but the nozzle was pointing at the pocket wall, not into the pocket.

## 4. Root cause

Tool wear combined with chip packing. The tool life counter was set too high for this material.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Tool life for this tool and material reduced by 30 %.
- Coolant nozzle position added to the setup sheet.

## 6. Lessons learned

When an overload repeats at the same program block, look at the process (tool, chips, feed), not at
the spindle.
