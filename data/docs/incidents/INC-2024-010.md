# INC-2024-010 M-08 discharge temperature rising over weeks — oil cooler fouled

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT (FabPilot demo). Author: utilities technician, M.F.

## 1. Summary

The discharge temperature of M-08 had been rising slowly for about six weeks and reached `TEMP-HIGH`
on hot afternoons. The inlet filter dp was normal (element only 3 weeks old). The reading was
confirmed by the mechanical thermometer. The oil cooler fins were clogged. Cleaned; temperature back
to the low 80s.

## 2. Timeline

- Mid-July to late August: `discharge_temp` daily maximum rising from about 86 to 96 °C; higher on hot
  days, lower at night.
- 26 Aug: `TEMP-HIGH` (96 °C) at 15:30.
- 27 Aug: `inlet_filter_dp` 22 mbar (normal). Mechanical thermometer agrees with the RTD (±1 °C).
  Cooler inspected: fins covered with a dusty oil film. Cleaned from the clean side.

## 3. Evidence

- Temperature rising over **weeks** and following the load and the outside temperature: a real
  cooling problem, not a drifting sensor (a drifting sensor doesn't care about the weather).
- Filter dp and current normal: not the inlet filter (MAN-CMP-001 §5.2).
- Reference thermometer confirms the RTD.

## 4. Root cause

Fouled oil cooler. A small oil leak at a fitting produced oil mist, which made dust stick to the fins.

## 5. Corrective actions

- Cooler cleaned; leaking fitting replaced.
- Cooler inspection moved from yearly to quarterly in summer.

## 6. Lessons learned

Three different causes give a high discharge temperature on this compressor: filter (moves with filter
dp), cooler (moves with weather and load over weeks), sensor (moves alone, also at standstill).
